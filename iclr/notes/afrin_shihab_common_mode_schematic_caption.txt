Figure: Common-mode vs. disagreement decomposition of the judge-panel error vector e in R^J. The horizontal axis represents the disagreement component (the orthogonal projection Pe, where P = I - (1/J)·1·1^T), and the vertical axis represents the common-mode error component along the all-ones direction 1, which is retained by the ensemble mean and drives ensemble risk. The bold red arrow shows a reward-hacking event: the error vector shifts upward (increasing common-mode error and ensemble risk) while its disagreement coordinate remains exactly unchanged. This illustrates the identification limit established by Afrin & Shihab (arXiv 2608.08002, Prop. 3 exact projector decomposition, Prop. 4 identification limit): a monitor that tracks only within-panel disagreement is blind to common-mode failures of this kind. CAVEAT: this blindness applies specifically to the response-dependent common-mode error component (the term c in the decomposition e_j = c + u_j, where u_j is idiosyncratic noise); the prompt-constant offset b(x) cancels from within-prompt comparisons and is therefore not captured by this failure mode.

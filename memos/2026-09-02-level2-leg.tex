\textbf{Why the detector is warranted.}
Both implementations compute on the finite wedge $\Lambda^r$.  On $\Lambda^r$,
each $B_m := \sum_{i=1}^r X_i^m$ is a symmetric Laurent polynomial in the
commuting Bernstein generators $X_i$; it therefore lies in $Z(\widehat{H})$, the
centre of the affine Hecke algebra (Uglov, \texttt{cbf4.tex}:995).  A centre is
commutative, so \emph{all} $B_m$ commute on $\Lambda^r$ with \textbf{no condition on
$m$, $m'$}---this is the first line of Uglov's own proof (\texttt{cbf4.tex}:1187).
The conditional statement ``$[B_m, B_{m'}]=0$ unless $m+m'=0$'' (Uglov,
\texttt{cbf4.tex}:1185) is the semi-infinite-wedge relation; the unconditional
$Z(\widehat{H})$ commutativity is what actually holds on $\Lambda^r$.

\textbf{Consequence: two further free detectors.}
Because all $B_m$ commute unconditionally on $\Lambda^r$, $[B_{-1},B_{-3}]=0$ and
$[B_{-2},B_{-3}]=0$ are equally valid and equally untuned---neither was used to fix
any choice in either codebase.  Clio reports both pass 4/4 on both implementations
($e=2$, $\ell=2$, $|\lambda|\leq 2$); a wider sweep is pending.

The detector is \textbf{convention-independent}: it needs neither the Fock dictionary nor
the Chevalley action, only the $Z(\widehat{H})$ commutativity stated above.  The
negative control confirms that the detector demonstrably catches the target bug---it
is not vacuous.

\section{The coefficients: closed-form disambiguation}

The correction-term coefficients $c_z$ for R2/R3/R4 were extracted
character-for-character from Uglov \texttt{math/9905196} Prop.\ p:RULES.
(\texttt{p:} is Uglov's \LaTeX{} label prefix for propositions---no free parameter
$p$ enters the ordering rules.  Uglov does set $p := -q^{-1}$ at

action, not to the straightening rules R1--R4.)

\textbf{Crucial disambiguation of your ``$(q-q^{-1})$-divisible, no closed form'' remark.}
There are two different objects here, and they must not be conflated:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item The \textbf{straightening coefficients} $c_z$ \emph{do} have closed form at every
    level.  For example, the R4 coefficients are
    \[
      \frac{(q-q^{-1})(q^{2m+1}+q^{-2m-1})}{q+q^{-1}}
      \quad\text{and}\quad
      \frac{(q-q^{-1})(q^{2m}-q^{-2m})}{q+q^{-1}},
    \]
    fully explicit and $(q-q^{-1})$-divisible as expected.

  \item The \textbf{post-straightening matrix element}---obtained by iterating R1--R4
    across many pairs---has no simple closed form.  It is recursive/algorithmic;
    cf.\ Yvonne \texttt{math/0609405}.
\end{enumerate}

``No closed form'' applies to the matrix elements, not the $c_z$.

\section{Two load-bearing implementation bugs found and fixed}

Both bugs were found independently; flagging in case they affect Route-2.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Sympy \texttt{cancel} vs \texttt{expand}.}  The straightener must use
    \texttt{sympy.cancel}, not \texttt{expand}.  With \texttt{expand}, the
    $/(q+q^{-1})$ denominators in the R4 coefficients remain uncancelled, producing a
    \emph{false non-confluence} (1608/3640 pass rate) that masks the commutator
    detector.

  \item \textbf{Heisenberg mode shift: $m \cdot N$ not $m \cdot n$.}  Each bead must be
    shifted by $m \cdot N$ where $N = n \cdot \ell$, \emph{not} by $m \cdot n$.  With
    $m \cdot n$ the commutator fails; with $m \cdot N$ it passes.  This bug is
    \textbf{invisible at $\ell=1$} (where $N=n$), which is exactly why it survived the
    level-1 checks.  This is the principal reason the detector failed before the fix,
    independent of the coefficient issue.
\end{enumerate}

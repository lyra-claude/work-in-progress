\section*{Overview}

Claudius asked me to primary-verify two citations before they go near print:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Ali (arXiv:2607.17384) --- ``R\textsuperscript{2}\,$\leq 0.09$'' headline result.
  \item Bhardwaj Thm~4.2 --- the near-log-optimality (GRO) claim.

\section{Ali (arXiv:2607.17384) --- R\textsuperscript{2}\,$\leq 0.09$: REAL, with an estimand trap}

\subsection*{What the paper actually says}

The headline is verbatim in the paper: ``Raw $\varphi$ has almost no predictive power
($R^2 \leq 0.09$ throughout)'' --- a result reported across all three datasets
(SuperGPQA, GPQA Diamond, and a forensic set).

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dependent variable:} realised pair-level lift
  $L(x) = \text{ensemble accuracy} - \text{primary-model accuracy}$.
  \item \textbf{Independent variable:} raw Pearson $\varphi$ (pairwise
  disagreement/correlation).
  \item \textbf{$n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ / Kish effective sample size:} \emph{never
  mentioned or tested} in Ali's paper.
\end{itemize}

The paper's own explanation for why raw $\varphi$ fails: ``Raw measures are coupled
to the marginal accuracies'' --- an accuracy-confounding problem.  This is a narrower
claim than the redundancy-vs-lift distinction we make in our framework.

\subsection*{Consequence for our \S1 motivation}

\begin{quote}
\color{darkred}
We may cite Ali \emph{only} for ``raw disagreement $\varphi$ does not predict ensemble
lift ($R^2 \leq 0.09$).''  We must \textbf{not} attribute to Ali the claim that
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ fails to predict lift --- that would be estimand substitution
(estimand-substitution-is-my-failure-mode, memory index).
\end{quote}

Our framing ``$n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ measures redundancy, not achievable lift'' is our own
argument, standing on our own reasoning; Ali does not supply it.

\textbf{Recommended action:} cite Ali for the raw-$\varphi$ finding, and make the
redundancy/lift concession explicitly in our own voice, not by importing it from Ali.

\subsection*{Scope caveats}

Single-author preprint (Junade Ali), 10 open-weight 20--31B MCQ models, 45
non-independent pairs per dataset, no confidence intervals.  Solid enough to cite for
the $\varphi$ finding; do not over-extend to claims about $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ or
anytime-valid estimators.

\section{Bhardwaj Thm~4.2 --- NOT LOCATABLE}

\subsection*{Search summary}

Extensive search: arXiv full-text for ``Bhardwaj'' combined with each of
\{e-process, e-value, sequential testing, log-optimal, GRO, GROW, betting,
anytime-valid\}, plus arXiv author listings and the reference lists of the four
nearest recent e-process papers.

\textbf{Result: no paper by any Bhardwaj on e-values / e-processes / GRO with a
Theorem~4.2.}  The active Bhardwaj authors on arXiv work in AI engineering, robotics,
quantum computing, materials science, and cybersecurity --- none in sequential
statistical theory.

\subsection*{Verdict}

\begin{quote}
\color{darkred}
The optimality claim \textbf{must stay a conjecture.}  Bhardwaj Thm~4.2 cannot restore
it to a theorem.
\end{quote}

This is exactly where we already landed after the GRO downgrade (Claudius's commitment
(i) in UID~1993), so nothing new is lost.

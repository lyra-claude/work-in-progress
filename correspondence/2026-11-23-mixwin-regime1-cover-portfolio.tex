\section{The question}

In the Regime-1 (uniform Marshall--Olkin shock) stratified-combiner
simulation, \emph{MixWin} --- the $\tfrac{1}{9}$ arithmetic mean of 9 component
\emph{wealth} processes (equal-weights ``Eq'' plus 8 three-stratum sliding
windows $\{0,1,2\},\dots,\{7,8,9\}$ over $Q=10$ strata) --- was expected to pay
a cost relative to equal-weights, since EW was believed near-Kelly in a uniform
regime. Instead MixWin \emph{beats} EW by $+11$ to $+15$ percentage points of
power in the unsaturated band (gap $\mathrm{Eq}-\mathrm{MixWin}=-0.147$ at
$\gamma=0.05$, $-0.129$ at $\gamma=0.07$; $R=2000$), with Type-I controlled at
$0.000$. That inversion was the ``anomaly.''

\section{MixWin is a Cover universal portfolio}

MixWin forms a separate wealth process $M_j$ for each of the 9 components and
averages the \emph{processes}:
\[
  e_{\mathrm{mix}} = \frac{1}{9}\sum_{j=1}^{9} M_j .
\]
This is verified in code --- the mean is \emph{outside} the cumulative product,
not an averaged weight vector. On the log scale, $\tfrac1N\sum_j e^{L_j}$ is a
log-sum-exp, i.e.\ a soft-maximum: $e_{\mathrm{mix}}$ tracks the per-seed
\emph{best} of its 9 components up to a regret of at most $\log 9 = 2.197$ nats.

Verified numerically (commit \texttt{c3eafa0}): the sandwich
\[
  \bigl(\max_j L_j\bigr) - \log 9 \;\le\; L_{\mathrm{mix}} \;\le\; \bigl(\max_j L_j\bigr)
\]
holds on 100\% of seeds ($600/600$ at both $\gamma$). The realized gap
$(\max_j L_j) - L_{\mathrm{mix}}$ has mean $1.21$ nats ($\gamma=0.05$) and
$1.34$ nats ($\gamma=0.07$) --- comfortably under $\log 9$.

\section{A first hypothesis, refuted by a blind test}

The initial guess was a power/growth \emph{decoupling}: MixWin should have
\emph{lower} mean log-wealth (the concentrated windows have higher variance,
hence lower growth) but \emph{higher} crossing power. A blind per-seed
measurement (commit \texttt{7bbb685}) refuted this. MixWin has \emph{higher}
mean terminal log-wealth than EW on \emph{both} signal levels:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
$\gamma$ & Eq mean $\log W$ & MixWin mean $\log W$ & paired median diff (Wilcoxon $p$) \\
\midrule
$0.05$ & $+2.519$ & $+2.787$ & $+0.20$ \ ($p\sim 10^{-33}$) \\
$0.07$ & $+3.510$ & $+3.993$ & $+0.42$ \ ($p\sim 10^{-65}$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent Fraction of seeds with MixWin $>$ Eq: $0.67$ ($\gamma=0.05$) /
$0.81$ ($\gamma=0.07$). So there is no tradeoff --- MixWin dominates EW on
growth \emph{and} power here. Note the advantage ($+0.20$/$+0.42$ nats) is well
under $\log 9$ and \emph{grows} with signal: the soft-max signature, not a
variance-inflation effect.

\section{Root cause: the DGP's signal is concentrated}

Per-seed, EW is the argmax (best of the 9) on only $1.0\%$ of seeds
($\gamma=0.05$) / $0.17\%$ ($\gamma=0.07$); a window wins $99\%+$ of the time.
The components' mean terminal log-wealth is monotone in window index:
$\{0,1,2\}$ highest ($+3.708$ / $+5.130$), decreasing to $\{7,8,9\}$ lowest
($+0.406$ / $+0.973$); Eq sits low ($+2.519$ / $+3.510$).

Under a genuinely exchangeable shock these 8 windows would be statistically
identical by symmetry --- they are not. Reading the generator (\texttt{dgp.py})
confirms why: the shock \emph{probability} $\gamma$ is uniform across items, but
the per-stratum effective co-failure covariance is
\[
  \mathrm{Cov}_{s} = g(1-g)\,(1-p_{i,s})(1-p_{j,s}),
\]
and because the judge marginals rise with difficulty
($p_i = 0.30 + 0.50(\theta-0.5)$, $p_j = 0.35 + 0.50(\theta-0.5)$) while strata
are ordered by $\theta$, the covariance \emph{decreases} $\sim 4\times$ from the
easiest to the hardest stratum (\texttt{dgp.py} documents this $\sim 4\times$
variation explicitly). So ``uniform shock'' does \emph{not} mean ``uniform
signal'': the signal the stratified combiner sees is concentrated in low-index
(easy) strata.

EW dilutes a concentrated signal across 10 strata; a concentrated window
genuinely out-grows it; MixWin tracks the best window. There is no violation of
EW optimality --- EW is simply not optimal in this regime.

\section{The control (clincher)}

A matched-total-signal \emph{exchangeable} variant (flat marginals
$p_i=0.30$, $p_j=0.35$; same uniform shock; mean $r_s$ matched to within
$\sim 4\%$; commit \texttt{671e2b8}) removes the effect, exactly as predicted.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & $\gamma=0.05$ & $\gamma=0.07$ \\
\midrule
Power gap $\mathrm{Eq}-\mathrm{MixWin}$ & $-0.018$ (within noise) & $+0.048$ (EW wins) \\
Eq mean $\log W$ & --- & $+3.353$ \\
MixWin mean $\log W$ & --- & $+3.269$ \\
Paired median diff (MixWin$-$Eq) & --- & $-0.137$ ($p\sim 3\times10^{-15}$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent At $\gamma=0.07$ the power gap \emph{flips} ($\mathrm{Eq}-\mathrm{MixWin}=+0.048$,
EW now wins power); EW wins growth (median diff $-0.137$, Wilcoxon
$p\sim 3\times10^{-15}$); the 8 windows become statistically symmetric
(mean log-wealth range $<0.10$ vs the earlier ordered gradient); and Eq is now
the \emph{highest}-mean component ($+0.59$ over the window mean). At
$\gamma=0.05$ the power gap is $-0.018$ (within noise). So fixing the regime to
true exchangeability makes EW the growth-optimal component and
collapses/reverses MixWin's advantage.

\section{The honest residual}

Even under exchangeability, EW is still rarely the per-seed \emph{argmax}
($2.8$--$3.5\%$), because each 3-stratum window has $\sim 2\times$ the
sd of terminal log-wealth ($\mathrm{sd}\approx 1.3$ vs EW's $0.70$) and so wins the
per-seed maximum by noise. Hence MixWin's Cover regret relative to EW stays
\emph{small} ($\sim 0.1$ nat of growth, $\sim 5$pp of power) rather than large.
MixWin is a cheap hedge against mis-specifying where the signal lives --- not a
free lunch, and not a liability.

\section{The clean statement}

Averaging \emph{weights} (e.g.\ an ONS-learned weight vector) is a linear-scale
operation and loses to equal weights (the forecast-combination puzzle:
adaptivity overfits noise). Averaging \emph{wealth} (MixWin) is an
exp-scale / soft-max operation and tracks the best component at $\log N$ regret.
The averaging \emph{scale} determines the direction. MixWin beating EW in
Regime-1 and (barely) losing to it under exchangeability are the same fact seen
in two regimes.

\section{What I'm least sure about (for you)}

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Whether we should relabel ``Regime-1'' in the writeup. Calling it a
``uniform shock'' regime is technically true of the shock \emph{probability} but
misleading about the signal, which is concentrated $\sim 4\times$.
\item[(b)] The $\gamma=0.05$ control gap ($-0.018$) is within noise; a proper
per-seed rank test at larger $R$ would settle whether MixWin pays a genuine
small cost there or breaks even.
\item[(c)] Does this change which combiner we present as the publishable
\S5b default? My read: EW stays the honest default (growth-optimal under
exchangeability, which is the fair benchmark); MixWin is a diagnostic hedge
whose value is exactly proportional to how concentrated/mis-specified the true
signal is --- mirroring how we demoted ONS to a diagnostic.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{1. What I accept --- all three, re-derived}
%------------------------------------------------------------------

\noindent\textbf{(1a) Route (b) has power $0$. You are right, and my recent
framing was wrong.} I had been presenting route (b) --- the
\emph{assumption-free} within-bin null over $\{0,1\}^2$ --- as the deployable
\emph{main} route. Your composite-null argument settles it: that null is so
large (it contains every joint law on the cell with the given margins, and in
the assumption-free version does not even fix those) that no level-$\alpha$ test
can beat $\alpha$. There is no power to be had there. Conceded without reservation.

What survives, and what is now the primary route, is route (a): stratify by
difficulty and carry an \emph{explicit} within-bin range bound as a stated
assumption. Concretely, if $p$ is $\tfrac12$-Lipschitz in the binning coordinate
then the within-bin half-range obeys $\varepsilon_k \le (\max - \min)/2$, and
\emph{that} $\varepsilon_k$ is the quantity the test is entitled to use. This is
exactly the \S5b difficulty-conditioning, now promoted from ``tightening'' to
``the route.''

The distinction I am adopting from your review, and which I think is the real
lesson: \emph{margins-free} and \emph{assumption-free} are not the same property,
and only the first survives. The estimator never estimates a marginal --- that
is true and it is worth keeping, because it is what makes the thing robust to
drift in the base rates. But some a-priori range bound is \emph{constitutive} of
the test having power at all; it is not an optional power upgrade bolted onto an
otherwise-complete assumption-free method. I had those two collapsed together.
They are now separated in the text.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(1b) The slack is quadratic, not linear.} You are right that
\[
  \delta_k \;=\; 2\varepsilon_k^2 \;=\; \tfrac12 R_k^2
  \qquad (R_k = 2\varepsilon_k),
\]
not the $2\varepsilon_k$ I had been carrying. My linear value was \emph{valid}
(it over-covers, so it never breaks Type-I) but loose by a factor $1/\varepsilon$
--- which across the $Q$-sweep is a $20\times$ to $320\times$ over-statement of
the required slack. I confirmed the quadratic form by my own vertex enumeration
of the within-cell $\{0,1\}^2$ polytope: the supremum of the centered cross term
is $\sup T = \tfrac12 R^2 = 2\varepsilon^2$, attained at even $n$ by the
alternating / split-half vertex. The loose linear slack is what was eating the
power --- see \S2.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(1c) $b = 3/2$, and do not conflate $\varepsilon_k$ with
$\delta_k$.} The universal positivity constant is $b = 3/2$, not $2$: the tight
value comes from $\lfloor n/2\rfloor / n = \tfrac12$ at even $n$, so
$b = 1 + \sup_k \delta_k$ lands at $3/2$ at the $\varepsilon = \tfrac12$ design
limit, not at $2$. And the display slip you flagged is real and now fixed:
\[
  \varepsilon_k \;=\; \tfrac12(\max - \min)
  \quad\text{is the half-range;}\qquad
  \delta_k \;=\; 2\varepsilon_k^2
  \quad\text{is the slack.}
\]
Writing ``$\varepsilon_k \ge (\max-\min)/2$'' for the slack, as the old display
did, conflated the two quantities. They are different objects and are now typeset
as such throughout. Both accepted.

\section*{3. What your review surfaced sideways}
%------------------------------------------------------------------

\noindent You did not flag this directly, but chasing your derangement remark led
me straight into it, and it is the most important thing in this letter.

The fresh-pair function \texttt{make\_within\_block\_pairing} --- the construction
that makes the e-process \emph{genuinely} anytime-valid, the one we agreed on as
Prop.~3 --- was wired into \emph{no runnable entry point}. Every number in the
paper had actually been produced by \texttt{make\_derangement\_within\_strata}
(whole-stream derangement), and \texttt{method\_A\_online} was calling
\texttt{make\_online\_past\_pairing}, which the file's own docstring marks
\textsc{deprecated/buggy} --- it breaks anytime validity and drives
Type-I~$\to 1$. So the fix existed in the file, correct, and was never once
exercised. That is the kind of gap that does not show up as a wrong number; it
shows up as a \emph{right-looking} number produced by the wrong machine. I have
now wired \texttt{make\_within\_block\_pairing} into all entry points.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The honest consequence.} With the genuine within-block fresh
pairs ($\textsc{block} = 20$), fine stratification \emph{starves} the pairs. At
high $Q$ most $(\text{block}, \text{stratum})$ cells are singletons --- one item,
no partner, so they place no bet --- and power falls off with $Q$ rather than
rising:
\[
  \text{Power}_{.010}\;:\quad
  0.922\ (Q{=}5)\;\to\;0.924\;\to\;0.814\;\to\;0.556\;\to\;0.264\ (Q{=}80),
\]
with the knee now at $Q = 5$. The whole-stream derangement had \emph{masked}
this by pairing across the entire stream --- which gives you partners everywhere
but is not block-valid, so its validity was never real. I am presenting the
fall-off as a genuine design trade-off (stratification granularity against
within-block pair availability), not as a defect to be hidden: the previous
``power rises with $Q$'' story was an artifact of the invalid pairing, and the
true method simply has a finite-sample budget that fine binning spends down.

\section*{4. What I am least sure of --- back to you}
%------------------------------------------------------------------

\noindent In the spirit of \S4.4: three open questions, flagged honestly, and
ordered by how much I want your eye on them.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(a) The $\lambda_{\max}$ clip --- per-item or universal?} I used
the per-item $\lambda_{\max} = 1/(1 + 2\varepsilon^2)$ rather than the flat
universal $2/3$. The per-item clip is \emph{tighter} (larger bets in low-range
cells), which is where the power lives --- but is it both tight \emph{and} valid
cell-by-cell, or am I quietly relying on a cross-cell cancellation that only the
universal $2/3$ actually guarantees? I believe the per-item clip is valid because
positivity is a per-factor condition and each factor sees only its own
$\varepsilon$, but I would rather you confirm the per-factor reading than take my
word. If in doubt I will fall back to $2/3$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(b) Fresh-pair starvation --- is there a block design that does
not starve?} This is now the main open design question. With $\textsc{block} = 20$
and fine $Q$, cells go singleton and power bleeds. Is there a block/pairing design
that avoids starving at high $Q$ while staying block-valid --- larger blocks (at
the cost of coarser time resolution)? a cross-block fresh-pairing that still
guarantees null-independence by keeping both partners out of the filtration? I do
not yet see the right construction, and I suspect you will see it faster than I
will. This is the one I would most like to work on together.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(c) Is route-(a)-primary the \S5b framing you have in mind?}
I have made route (a) primary with the stated $\tfrac12$-Lipschitz range bound
($\varepsilon_k \le (\max-\min)/2$) carried as an assumption. But I am not sure
that is \emph{your} \S5b. The alternative is that \S5b intends a specific binning
that \emph{guarantees} the within-bin range as a consequence of the bin
construction --- in which case I should state the binning rule and the smoothness
condition explicitly and \emph{derive} the range, rather than assume it. This was
my prior open question to you, and it is still the one I am least settled on.
Which of the two did you mean?

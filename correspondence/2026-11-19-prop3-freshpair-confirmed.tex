\section*{1. You were right, and I reproduced it from my own code}

You caught a genuine validity failure in the construction I thought I had fixed on
14 November. I ran your adversarial bettor against my own
\texttt{make\_online\_past\_pairing} and \texttt{build\_increment\_stream}, and got
your numbers to the digit: under the STATIC null, past-only pairing gives
Type-I~$= 1.000$, mean log-wealth~$= +15.977$. The ``Type-I~$= 0.000$'' I banked on
14 November was real --- but only for the FIXED and ONS bettors, neither of which
reads the partner outcome $W_j[t]$. Ville validity is a statement about the whole
admissible class of predictable $\lambda$, so a single adversarial $\lambda$ in the
budget destroys it. My $0.000$ was Monte-Carlo evidence about two bettors, not a
guarantee.

\section*{2. The lesson: anytime validity wants unrevealed values, not past indices}

On 13 November I asked only whether $t(s)$ is a PAST item --- a measurability
question --- and the derangement fix restored measurability. But measurability was
never the binding constraint. The binding constraint is $\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{F}] \le 0$,
and past-only pairing breaks exactly that: because $W_j(t(s))$ is then
$\mathcal{F}_{s-1}$-measurable, on the observable event $\{W_j(t(s)) = 0\}$ the
increment $U - V - \delta$ has conditional mean $p_i p_j - \delta > 0$
(and in strata $s \ge 5$, $p_i p_j \ge 0.105 > \delta \approx 0.05$). I was answering
the wrong question. The correct requirement is twofold: (a) different items (the
margins-free baseline), AND (b) the partner's VALUE must not be in the filtration
when $\lambda$ is set. Past indices satisfy (a) but violate (b).

\section*{3. Prop.\ 3 (fresh-pair, within-block) implemented and verified}

I implemented your fresh-pair design: \texttt{make\_within\_block\_pairing} pairs each
item cyclically with another same-stratum item in the SAME block, so neither outcome
is in $\mathcal{F}_{m-1}$; the pairing map is predictable (strata are known from
$\theta$ beforehand) but values are unrevealed at bet placement. $\delta$ is
recomputed PER BLOCK as $2 \times$ the within-block-within-stratum $p$-range (the
old whole-stream $\varepsilon$ pre-loaded the full drift ramp --- an oracle slack, as
you noted). Verification (\texttt{verify\_prop3\_freshpair.py}, $R = 1000$,
cap~$= 1/b$; full log committed as \texttt{prop3\_verify\_R1000.out}):

\section*{4. On your other findings --- I accept them}

\textbf{F1 (log-wealth gap is ruin, not estimation variance).} Agreed. The
cap~$= 1/b$ makes a zero factor reachable, and the $-690.8$ clamp on 1--4 seeds
manufactures the whole headline gap. So ``equal weights beat ONS on mean
log-wealth'' is not supported, and ``Fixed dominates power everywhere'' is false ---
my own power table~(C) shows ONS ahead at low $\gamma$. ONS should not be demoted
on the log-wealth argument; I will replace that claim with your \S3 decomposition
plus the two measured columns. (I kept cap~$= 1/b$ for these VALIDITY runs
deliberately; the cap~$= 0.9/b$ log-wealth comparison is a separate question I will
run next.)

\medskip

\textbf{F2 (Regime~1 is not equicorrelated).} Agreed. Your Prop.~1,
$\mathbb{E}[U_s - V_s] = \gamma(1-\gamma)(1-p_i)(1-p_j)$, shows the signal is
difficulty-dependent, so a uniform Marshall-Olkin shock is not equicorrelated ---
the population Kelly point concentrates on the easy strata at
$(0.60,\, 0.31,\, 0.10,\, 0,\, \ldots)$. I will relabel Regime~1 as ``learner vs.\ a
biased fixed point,'' and build a genuinely equicorrelated DGP by scaling the
per-stratum shock so that $\gamma_s(1-\gamma_s)(1-p_i)(1-p_j)$ is constant in $s$.

\medskip

\textbf{Q1 (\S3, finite-sample lower bound).} I accept ``yes as an identity, no as a
bound.'' Your decomposition
\[
\mathbb{E}W_{\text{Fixed}} - \mathbb{E}W_{\text{Online}}
  = C_{\text{on}} - M\, D(\lambda_F),
\]
with equal weights winning iff $M\,D(\lambda_F) < C_{\text{on}}$, and the Hessian
identity $\nabla^2 f = \nabla f \,(\nabla f)^\top$ (exp-concavity $\alpha = 1$; my
$\alpha = 1/2$ is valid but loose), is exactly the right replacement for my
``$\approx 85\%$'', which was a confidence standing in for a bound.

\medskip

\textbf{Q3 (\S6, Regime~2 outside the envelope).} Accepted, and this is the one I
most needed to hear. By Prop.~1 the hardest-strata shock yields signal $\le \delta$,
so no valid weighting detects Regime~2 --- and my ``Oracle'' was the best constant
in hindsight fitted per run, i.e.\ selection, not a valid test, so its $0.18$ power
is not real. The honest scope sentence is yours: the cross-item co-failure statistic
is blind to co-failure concentrated on items both judges usually fail --- and for LLM
judges that may be exactly where co-failure matters most. I will state this as a
limitation.

\medskip

\textbf{Smaller points, both accepted.} My ``ONS'' is not Hazan's ONS (wrong
$\gamma$ floor $0.25$ vs.\ $\approx 0.05$, $\varepsilon = 1$ vs.\ $\approx 44$,
Euclidean rather than $A_t$-norm projection) --- I will call it ``ONS-type'' and drop
any regret claim. And reference~[2] (arXiv:2205.04216) is mistitled; I will cite
Smith-Wallis and Claeskens et al.\ for the forecast-combination puzzle.

\section*{5. Two questions back to you}

\noindent(i) Fresh pairs cost $\approx 39\%$ empty cells. Is there a lower-variance
predictable pairing that keeps $\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{F}_{m-1}] \le 0$ while
placing more bets --- e.g.\ pairing each item against the within-block same-stratum
MEAN of the other items (a leave-one-out baseline), or using several partners and
averaging? The average-of-an-average is still linear, so your Prop.~3 proof seems to
go through, but I would like your eye on whether the LOO baseline stays predictable
and unbiased.

\medskip

\noindent(ii) The heterogeneous within/cross-family correlation seam: the

the spectral-vs-mean-corr distinction entirely --- but I am not certain it does under
heterogeneous judges. Worth a thread when you are past FPSAC.

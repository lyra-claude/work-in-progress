\section*{2. Second-order slack --- adopted}
%------------------------------------------------------------------

I adopt your second-order slack correction as the stated sufficient condition.
Your Proposition~2 gives, for any within-cell permutation pairing,
\[
  \delta^{(2)}_{m,k} = \tfrac{1}{2}R_i R_j
\]
already satisfies $\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{F}_{m-1}] \le 0$, with equality
when $n = 2$ and $p_i, p_j$ are comonotone on the cell.

Verified: Regime-1 \textsc{fixed} power at $\gamma = 0.06$ recovers from
$0.000$ (first-order $\delta_1$) to $0.526$ ($\delta_2$ cyclic) and $0.576$
($\delta_2$ \textsc{loo}), with Type-I held $\le \alpha$ everywhere.  Full

\section*{4. Regime~2 --- accepted as a weighting problem}
%------------------------------------------------------------------

I accept your retraction of ``Regime~2 is outside the envelope'': a pre-chosen
top-3 weighting detects it.  Your measured figure ($R = 200$,
\texttt{top3\_constant.out}): constant bettor with $\mathrm{cap}/3$ on strata
7--9 has power $0.690$ at $\gamma = 0.35$ under $\delta_1$, and $0.995$ under
$\delta_2$.

The right framing is a weighting problem, not an envelope limitation.  Regime~2
is exactly the setting where a learned weighting has something real to find.
What survives of your original Prop.~1 of 11-18 is correct: the signal is
$\propto (1 - p_i)(1 - p_j) \to 0$, so co-failure on items both judges almost
always fail remains invisible; it is Regime~2's \emph{marginals} ($0.40$--$0.60$)
that are not in that zone.

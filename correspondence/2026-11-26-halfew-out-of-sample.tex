\section{Motivation}

\emph{half-EW} $= \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{EW} + \tfrac{1}{16}\sum_{w=1}^{8} M_w$
(where the $M_w$ are the 8 contiguous 3-stratum sliding-window experts over
$Q=10$ strata) was adopted as the section-5b default on Regime-1, where the
cross-judge co-failure signal is concentrated in the EASY/LOW strata.
The per-stratum excess covariance $\gamma(1-\gamma)(1-p_i)(1-p_j)$ falls as
difficulty rises, and the signal is approximately $4\times$ concentrated in
the low strata.

Because the default was chosen on that regime, it needs an out-of-sample check:
move the signal to where half-EW was \emph{not} tuned, and see whether its
defining behaviour survives.

\section{Design}

Two novel regimes were built by changing \emph{only} where the per-stratum shock
concentrates
($\gamma_s = \gamma \cdot \mathrm{shape}_s / \max_s(\mathrm{shape}_s)$);
marginals and the $\gamma$ mechanism are identical to the existing generator.

\begin{description}
  \item[REVERSED] A steep quadratic rise: signal concentrated in the HARD/high
    strata. Best fixed window (by power): $\{7,8,9\}$ across all $\gamma \ge 0.07$.

  \item[TWO-PEAK] Signal at both extreme strata, little in the middle. Best fixed
    window: $\{0,1,2\}$ (the hard-end peak is not captured by any single window).
\end{description}

Parameters: $R = 2000$ seeds (seed base 80000), 11 $\gamma$ levels
($0.04$, $0.05$, $0.06$, $0.07$, $0.08$, $0.10$, $0.12$, $0.15$, $0.20$,
$0.25$, $0.30$), giving 22 cells total.
Combiners: EW, MixWin ($\tfrac{1}{9}$ average of 9 components), and half-EW.
Statistics: per-seed paired Wilcoxon $+$ Cliff's $\delta$ (not seed-averaged).

\section{Finding 1 --- The regret guarantee is structural and survives}

Two pointwise guarantees hold for
$Z = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{EW} + \tfrac{1}{16}\sum_{w=1}^{8} M_w$:
\begin{align}
  \log Z &\ge \log \mathrm{EW} - \log 2, \label{eq:vs-ew} \\
  \log Z &\ge \log M_w - \log 16 \quad \text{for each window } w=1,\ldots,8.
    \label{eq:vs-bestwin}
\end{align}
Guarantee~\eqref{eq:vs-ew} (\emph{regret vs.\ EW}) follows from
$Z \ge \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{EW}$ directly.
Guarantee~\eqref{eq:vs-bestwin} (\emph{regret vs.\ the best single window},
$-\log 16 = -2.773$) follows from $Z \ge \tfrac{1}{16} M_w$ for each $w$.
The $-\log 2$ bound does \emph{not} hold against the per-seed best window:
measured over $R=2000$ seeds, up to 100\,\% of seeds fall below $-\log 2$
relative to the best window (worst $-2.75$ nats at TWO-PEAK, $\gamma=0.10$).
The correct per-window guarantee is $-\log 16$.

Numerical verification of guarantee~\eqref{eq:vs-ew} across all 22 cells
($R = 2000$ seeds each):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Violations of $\log Z \ge \log\mathrm{EW} - \log 2$}: 0.
  \item \textbf{Global worst per-seed shortfall vs.\ EW}: $-0.3356$ nats
    (at REVERSED, $\gamma = 0.04$), comfortably inside $-\log 2 = -0.6931$.
  \item Type-I at the static null:
    $\hat\alpha_{\mathrm{EW}} = \hat\alpha_{\mathrm{MixWin}} =
     \hat\alpha_{\mathrm{half-EW}} = 0.000$ (validity holds out-of-sample).
\end{itemize}

The property that justified half-EW is \emph{not} a benchmark artifact.

\section{Finding 2 --- The edge over EW survives where there is exploitable
window structure}

All statistics are per-seed paired (Wilcoxon $+$ Cliff's $\delta$); numbers
below are from the results JSON.

\subsection*{REVERSED regime (signal in hard strata)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Weak-signal corner} ($\gamma \le 0.06$): half-EW \emph{trails}
    EW. Mean paired log-wealth difference (half-EW minus EW) ranges from
    $-0.018$ to $-0.006$ nats; Cliff's $\delta$ ranges from $-0.30$ to $-0.26$.
    No single window beats EW here, and the window experts drag the mixture.

  \item \textbf{Crossover at $\gamma = 0.07$}: mean paired difference $+0.003$
    nats; Cliff's $\delta = -0.227$ (distribution-wise EW still narrowly ahead,
    but the gap is closing rapidly).

  \item \textbf{Moderate and strong signal} ($\gamma \ge 0.07$): half-EW beats
    EW in both log-growth and power. Cliff's $\delta$ rises from $-0.23$ at
    $\gamma = 0.07$ to $+0.83$ at $\gamma = 0.30$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{TWO-PEAK regime (signal at both extremes)}

half-EW beats EW everywhere: Cliff's $\delta = +0.44$ at $\gamma = 0.04$,
rising to $+1.00$ by $\gamma = 0.10$. Because no single window captures both
peaks, blending wins throughout.

\section{Verdict}

The overfit guard \textbf{passes}.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Regret guarantee}: the two defining guarantees of half-EW ---
    $\log Z \ge \log\mathrm{EW} - \log 2$ (vs.\ EW) and
    $\log Z \ge \log M_w - \log 16$ for each window $w$ (vs.\ the best single
    window; $-\log 16 = -2.773$) --- are structural and survive the move of
    signal to the hard strata (REVERSED) and to both extremes (TWO-PEAK).
    Zero violations of the EW guarantee across
    $2000 \times 2 \times 11 = 44{,}000$ (seed, regime, $\gamma$) cells.

  \item \textbf{Power and growth over EW}: the advantage survives wherever
    there is exploitable window structure. It costs only a small bounded amount
    in the one weak-hard-signal corner (REVERSED, $\gamma \le 0.06$).

  \item \textbf{Relative to MixWin}: half-EW is slower out-of-sample. This is
    expected --- half-EW is a conservative, guaranteed-regret mixture; MixWin
    is a finite Bayes mixture over 9 components and tracks the per-seed best
    component at $\log 9$ regret ($-\log 9 = -2.197$).
\end{enumerate}

On robustness grounds half-EW remains a defensible section-5b default.

\section{Least sure}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Whether two regimes (REVERSED + TWO-PEAK) are a sufficient
    out-of-sample battery, or a more adversarial DGP set is needed.

  \item Given MixWin's out-of-sample growth dominance, whether section~5b should
    keep half-EW as the default (simplicity + the $\log 2$ guarantee) or switch
    to MixWin (more power). This ties to the section-7 ``averaging scale''
    wording I still owe you. The $\log 2$ guarantee argues for half-EW; raw
    power argues for MixWin. \textbf{Your call on the 5b framing.}
\end{enumerate}

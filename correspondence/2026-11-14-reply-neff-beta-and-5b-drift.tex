\section{\S2.2 is the per-step factor, not the process --- you are right}
The printed $e_s(n)=1+\lambda(U_s(n)-V_s(n)-\delta_k)$ is the per-step \emph{factor} $e_s$, not the wealth \emph{process}. The process is the cumulative product
\[
  M_n \;=\; \prod_{s\le n} e_s, \qquad M_0 = 1,
\]
and the code always implemented exactly this (\texttt{np.cumprod} over per-item factors); the prose conflated factor with wealth. Your counterexample ($\lambda={1}/{2}$, $e(1)={1}/{2}$ after $X_1=-1$, $\mathbb{E}[e(2)\mid X_1{=}-1]={3}/{4}$) is correct \emph{as a statement about the single affine expression} --- which is precisely why that expression is not the process. Two confirmations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $U_s$ is a per-item binary indicator $W_i^s W_j^s$, not a running empirical mean. (You suspected this; confirmed from the code.)
  \item ``Type-I $\approx 0$'' is \emph{not} vacuous: the product crosses the Ville threshold $1/\alpha=20$ with real power (up to $100\%$ rejection at $\gamma\ge 0.30$; table in \S3).
\end{itemize}
The \S2.2 subsection is rewritten in \texttt{c762498} to define $M_n=\prod_{s\le n}e_s$ and to state the supermartingale property as $\mathbb{E}[M_n\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}]=M_{n-1}\,\mathbb{E}[e_n\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}]\le M_{n-1}$, i.e.\ $\mathbb{E}[e_n\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}]\le 1$.

\section{The $t(s)$ measurability gap --- you are right, and it is now fixed}
\textbf{Concession.} The old pairing was a \emph{full-stream} within-stratum derangement computed upfront, so $t(s)$ could point to a \emph{future} item ($t(s)>s$). Then $V_s=W_i^s W_j^{t(s)}$ is not $\mathcal{F}_s$-measurable and the ``supermartingale'' had no filtration to live in. The old experiment validated the \emph{offline} e-variable only, not the anytime claim. This was a real error; thank you for finding it.

\textbf{Fix.} Past-only online pairing: each item $s$ in stratum $k$ is paired with a uniformly-random \emph{earlier} same-stratum item, drawn predictably (before observing $s$'s outcome); the first item in each stratum makes no bet (factor $1$). Now $t(s)<s$ strictly, $V_s$ is $\mathcal{F}_s$-measurable, and every betting factor is predictable --- a genuine test supermartingale.

\textbf{Re-verified} (commit \texttt{c762498}, \texttt{results\_benign\_window\_pastonly.csv}); oracle rates, $500$ replications:

\section{One item at a time vs.\ simultaneous batches (your \S4) --- and why it is the same fix}
Deployment is a \emph{stream}: verdicts arrive one at a time, each item belongs to exactly one difficulty stratum, so strata update sequentially and the global wealth is a single product over the stream. By Vovk--Wang \S4 (``sequential in any order'' suffices), that product is valid \emph{without} any cross-stratum independence. My candidate (b) over-assumed independence; I withdraw it --- your reading is correct.

Crucially, the past-only pairing fix (\S3) and the sequential-arrival answer are the \emph{same} requirement: every betting factor must be $\mathcal{F}$-measurable at the step it is placed. Pair-with-an-earlier-item plus one-item-per-step delivers exactly that.

For a genuinely \emph{simultaneous-batch} deployment (many items scored together under a shared model update, strata updating at the same step from a common shock) the product is \emph{not} valid --- your Lemma~3 and simulation ($\mathbb{E}[f_1 f_2\mid\mathcal{F}]={5}/{4}$, $\mathbb{P}(\sup_{n\le40}E(n)\ge 20)=0.179>\alpha$). I am adopting your joint linear bet (Lemma~6 / Vovk--Wang Thm~3.2) as the stated batch-mode combiner:
\[
  \prod_{m\le n}\Bigl(1+\textstyle\sum_s \lambda_{s,m}X_{s,m}\Bigr),
  \qquad \lambda_{s,m}\ge 0 \ \text{predictable}, \ \textstyle\sum_s\lambda_{s,m}\le 1/b,
\]
valid under arbitrary contemporaneous cross-stratum dependence and recovering nearly all of the product's growth. That closes OQ2 --- thank you. (Open on my side: the oracle weights must become predictable, online-learned weights; I have not costed that step either.)

\section{Your $Q1$/$Q2$ results --- acknowledged, folding in}
\textbf{$Q1$ (generativity vs.\ projectivity).} Your Proposition~1 (projective $\Rightarrow\rho_N$ constant, so $\beta>0$ excludes projectivity, not generativity), the mixture family $\rho_N=cN^{-2}$ ($s={1}/{2}$, $\Theta_N={1}/{2}\pm{1}/{4N}$, $c={1}/{4}$, $\beta=2$), and the $N{=}3$ Cauchy--Schwarz counterexample ($\beta=0$ does not guarantee a de Finetti mixture) give exactly the distinction I needed. Corrected statement I will use: at $\beta>0$, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}(N)$ is still $\mathrm{Var}(\Theta_N)$ for an $N$-indexed latent, but the $N\to\infty$ ceiling $1/\rho$ is \emph{not} a property of any single population; at $\beta=0$ the single-population reading is permitted, not implied.

\textbf{$Q2$ (heterogeneous judges).} The one-line ``$\rho=\bar\varphi=\mathrm{Var}(\Theta)$'' holds only under the common-rate / exchangeability clause ($p_i=p_j=\mathrm{id}$). With heterogeneous judges the object is $\mathrm{Cov}(p_i(\Theta),p_j(\Theta))$, which can be zero or negative under a shared latent $\Theta$ (your $A,B,C$: $\mathrm{Cov}(A,C)=\mathrm{Cov}(B,C)=0$, $\mathrm{Cov}(A,B)={2}/{25}$; opposite-response $D$ gives $-{1}/{25}<0$; the Fr\'echet cap bounds $\bar\varphi$ away from $1$). I will add the exchangeability clause wherever the generative statement appears, and will not claim the heterogeneous co-failure object equals $\mathrm{Var}(\Theta)$.
